# Devise the outline

Read the complete corrected transcript and any reference material before proposing chapters. Build an outline that follows the lecture's actual reasoning and time order. The outline is a planning aid, not a rigid chapter-count target.

## Internal classification

For every meaningful span of the transcript, classify its role internally using one or more of these macro-categories. Store the classification in the outline or working notes when useful, but do not expose the table in the final lesson unless the user asks for the analysis.

| Macro-category | What to identify | How it affects the outline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope and authority | What the lecture actually claims, assumes, or leaves open | Keep chapters inside the lecture's evidentiary and pedagogical boundary |
| Objective | The learner's intended new capability or understanding | Give each chapter and major concept a concrete purpose |
| Progression | How the material advances through mechanism, constraint, trade-off, evidence, application, or review | Put concepts in an order where each new idea has the needed foundation |
| Core substance | Definitions, mechanisms, procedures, examples, comparisons, and conclusions | Group related material and avoid fragmenting a connected argument |
| Evidence | Claims tied to a source page, quotation, measurement, example, or observation | Preserve provenance and plan citations where they are genuinely needed |
| Boundary and non-repetition | What a prior chapter establishes and what later material must add | Give later chapters a concise reference point instead of re-teaching the same thing |
| Uncertainty and limits | Ambiguity, exceptions, caveats, unresolved questions, and source conflicts | Keep uncertainty visible and prevent unsupported certainty |
| Presentation shape | Whether the material calls for prose, a list, a comparison table, an equation, or a visual | Choose the smallest structure that makes the relationship clear |

Use the transcript as the authority for substance and depth. Use references to stabilize terminology, fill a directly relevant detail, or support a citation. Resolve conflicts internally; do not surface an avoidable disagreement to the learner.

## Chapter boundaries

Choose boundaries at changes in objective, mechanism, or argumentative direction. A chapter may contain several related concepts, and a single concept may deserve several subsections. Do not force one chapter per concept or one model call per chapter. The caller or agent may choose a larger or smaller writing unit according to context length, continuity, and the material's coherence.

For each chapter or writing unit, retain:

- a topical title and short purpose;
- the transcript time span and the source excerpts it owns;
- the concepts and their intended progression;
- claims already established by earlier units;
- relevant reference documents and page ranges;
- citation targets and unresolved uncertainties.

Once the outline is coherent and covers the source timeline, continue to [write-chapters.md](write-chapters.md).
